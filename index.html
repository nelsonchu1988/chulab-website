    Featured Research · Nature Biomedical Engineering 2026

    Seeing inside intact human bone

    We developed a tissue-clearing and lightsheet pipeline that renders intact human bone biopsies optically transparent — preserving native 3D architecture while enabling deep imaging of proteins and mRNAs. The result is the first quantitative 3D atlas of the human osteovascular niche.

    What We Do

    Research Directions

    Three connected projects — basic, translational and clinical — share one engine: 3D molecular imaging of intact bone, single-cell sequencing and mouse genetics.

    01

    Cell Fate: Cartilage to Bone

    Hypertrophic chondrocytes in the growth plate can survive and become bone-forming osteoblasts. We ask what decides this switch, and how it could be used to improve bone-building drugs and fracture repair.

    02

    Growth Hormone and the Growth Plate

    Growth hormone acts directly on growth-plate stem cells. We study how growth-hormone treatments, including new weekly long-acting forms, affect these stem cells and metabolism — to make growth treatment for children safer.

    03

    Skeletal Disease in 3D

    We map diseased human bone, such as osteonecrosis of the femoral head, cell by cell in 3D, and extend rapid 3D molecular pathology to mineralised tissue.

    04

    Platform: 3D Molecular Imaging

    DeepBone combines tissue clearing, whole-mount RNA and protein labelling, light-sheet microscopy and registration to micro-CT, analysed together with single-cell sequencing data.

    Principal Investigator

    Nelson Chu, PhD

    Researcher · Department of Rheumatology and Inflammation Research
    Institute of Medicine, University of Gothenburg, Sweden

    I study how the skeleton grows, ages and fails in disease — at the scale of single cells inside intact tissue. My work brings together endocrinology (how growth hormone and PTH act on bone), developmental biology (growth-plate stem cells and the cartilage-to-bone cell-fate switch) and 3D molecular imaging of human bone.

    In Science Translational Medicine (2026) we built a transcriptional atlas of the pubertal human growth plate and showed that growth hormone acts directly on cartilage stem cells, building on our work in PNAS (2025). In Nature Biomedical Engineering (2026) I developed DeepBone, a tissue-clearing and light-sheet pipeline for 3D RNA and protein imaging of intact human bone, and used it to compare the osteovascular niche of young and aged bone.

    I trained in developmental genetics at the University of Hong Kong (PhD with Kathryn Cheah) and in growth-plate endocrinology at Karolinska Institutet (postdoc with Andrei Chagin), and now lead an independent line on 3D molecular imaging of human bone in Gothenburg.

    Our Toolkit

    Technologies

    We combine 3D imaging of intact tissue with single-cell genomics and mouse genetics to produce integrated, multimodal datasets of the skeleton.

    01 3D Molecular Imaging Tissue clearing (DeepBone) · Whole-mount RNA (HCR) & immunostaining · Light-sheet microscopy
    02 Multimodal Registration Micro-CT · Light-sheet ↔ CT registration · 3D quantification
    03 Single-Cell Genomics scRNA-seq · Trajectory & RNA velocity
    04 Gene Networks & Cell Communication SCENIC · CellChat · hdWGCNA
    05 Mouse Genetics Lineage tracing (Col10a1-CreERT2, Confetti) · Conditional knockouts
    06 Human Tissue & Explants Growth-plate & bone biopsies · Ex vivo explant culture
